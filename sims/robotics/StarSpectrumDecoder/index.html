STAR SPECTRUM DECODER

  1. Pick a Wand Colour — Blue, Sky Blue, White, Yellow, Orange or Red. The tip lights up, but nothing is read yet — the sensor only sees light once you start the scan.
  2. Set the LED Intensity (0–100%). 100% gives the full reference reading. Dim it and the signal and graph dim with it, down to a small sensor noise floor instead of zero.
  3. Turn Background Interference On or Off. On washes the scene bright with stray light and makes the graph glitch. Off keeps a clean, dark reading.
  4. Set the Sensor Sensitivity (0–100%). At 100% the graph is normal and clean. Lower it and the graph shrinks and gets shakier.
  5. Press Start under the decoder view. The wand swishes through the air and points at the sensor lens. The Decoder Status bar shows what is happening.
  6. Watch the AS7341 channel graph on the decoder console. It plots channels F1–F8 once a star is identified. The tallest points are what the controller compares against its colour map.
  7. Read Spectral Reading & Decode Status: the Wand Colour, Brightness, Interference, Dominant Channel and Star Result. Use the Star Mapping Reference to see which colour matches which star, from Rigel (blue) to Betelgeuse (red).
  8. Try dimming the LED Intensity right down and scanning again — the reading comes back too weak to classify. Then turn interference on: the star is still decoded, but the graph glitches while it reads.
  9. Press Run Guided Decode to watch all six colours scan one after another and fill in the Constellation Progress. System Feedback tells you when the hidden constellation is restored.
  10. Press Reset to start again. Change one setting at a time and compare.
  • Light as a signal: the colour of an LED can carry information, like a message sent with light.
  • Spectral sensing: a spectral sensor such as the AS7341 splits incoming light into channels (F1–F8) and measures each one.
  • Reading a spectrum: the readings can be drawn as a line graph, and the tallest channel shows the main colour.
  • Intensity: brighter light gives a stronger, clearer reading. Too dim, and the sensor cannot tell the colour.
  • Noise floor: even in darkness a sensor never reads exactly zero — there is always a little noise.
  • Clipping: a channel already at the sensor's maximum stays stuck at the top until the light dims enough to show its real value.
  • Interference: stray background light can confuse colour detection.
  • Colour mapping: the controller turns sensor readings into a star's identity by matching them to a colour map.
  • Careful measurement: scientific decoding needs careful observation, and changing one thing at a time.

Star Mapping Reference

Blue-dominant channels → Rigel (Blue Star)
Saturated blue-white → Sirius (Sky Blue Star)
Balanced, bright channels → Procyon (White Star)
Yellow-warm dominant → the Sun (Yellow Star)
Orange-warm dominant → Arcturus (Orange Star)
Red-dominant channels → Betelgeuse (Red Star)
Sirius and Procyon read almost identically to the AS7341 — both are bright enough to saturate most channels, a real sensor limitation, not a bug.
