1. Pick a mode at the top of the Web Dashboard, then press ▶ Start on the rover view.
  2. Rover buttons (FORWARD, BACKWARD, LEFT, RIGHT) keep the rover moving; LEFT and RIGHT curve it while it drives, until you press STOP or another button. ROVER_SPEED sets how fast. On a keyboard: hold the arrow keys or W A S D to drive; Space is STOP.
  3. Arm sliders send an exact angle to one servo: BASE, SHOULDER, ELBOW, WRIST, WRIST_ROTATION and GRIPPER. Each slider moves the way its joint moves (labels under it show which way); − / + nudge it 1° at a time, hold to keep going. HOME_ARM lifts the gripper clear, then folds the arm into its safe travel pose.
  4. Drop zones: you can pick an object up anywhere, but the gripper only opens to release it over a designated drop zone (the green Drop Zone, or the sample tray in Arm Slider mode).
  5. Arena & course: choose Salvage bay, Slalom, Gates or Open bay. ✏ Edit obstacles (before you press Start) lets you add barrels, crates, cones, barriers and metal walls, or remove them, and 📍 Move the pick-up zone, the drop zone, checkpoints A, B, C and the salvage items. Your layout is remembered on this computer; ↺ Reset positions puts the zones and items back.
  6. Every command travels Dashboard → Wi-Fi → ESP32 → motor driver or servo. Watch the Communication Path and the Response delay on the right.
  7. Picking up: drive until the Pickup distance meter says In reach, press STOP, turn the BASE toward the object, point the gripper down with the WRIST, turn the jaws with WRIST_ROTATION, open the GRIPPER, lower with the SHOULDER and close the GRIPPER.
  8. Carrying: lift the object first, then drive slowly. Fast starts, hard stops and crashes shake it loose.
  9. Battery: driving and moving the arm use power. Park on the Base Station pad and stop to recharge.
  10. ⏹ Stop ends the run and opens the result report; ↺ Reset puts the rover and arm back in a safe state and starts the mode again.
  11. Drag the 3D view to orbit, scroll to zoom. 👁 View switches between following the rover and the whole arena. Hover a rover part to see what it is.
  • One dashboard, two control systems: buttons give the rover a direction; sliders give each arm servo an exact angle.
  • Wi-Fi and the ESP32: the ESP32 runs a small web server. Each button press or slider value is sent over Wi-Fi; a weak signal adds delay and loses some commands, and with no signal nothing arrives.
  • Failsafe: if the ESP32 hears nothing from the dashboard for about a second, it stops the motors so the rover cannot run away.
  • Motor driver: the ESP32 cannot power a DC motor directly. It tells the motor driver which way to turn each motor (IN pins HIGH / LOW) and how hard (PWM on the EN pin). Steering = one side slower than the other, so the rover curves toward the slow side.
  • Servos: a servo goes to the angle it is sent and holds it. Six servos give the arm six degrees of freedom.
  • Rover position decides reach: the arm can only reach a ring around its base. Park in that ring, stop, and only then move the arm and grip.
  • Mobile manipulators like this one are used for rescue, repair and salvage work where a fixed arm could not reach.
