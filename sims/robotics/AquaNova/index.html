AQUANOVA

  1. Drive the rover. It starts in Patrol Mode, sweeping the habitat on its own. Hold ◀ / A or D / ▶ to take the wheel and drive it along the floor. Press P or Patrol Mode to hand it back.
  2. Spring a leak. Four pipes run across the ceiling — A B C D. Open any of them under Ceiling Pipes, or click a pipe on the ceiling, then raise the Drip Rate (0–100%). Each open pipe grows its own puddle in its own part of the habitat.
  3. Go and find the water. The rover only reads the puddle it is standing in, so open a far pipe and drive over to it. A dry floor between two puddles reads dry.
  4. Fill the habitat. Press ＋ Add for more creatures, up to six, or ✕ Clear to remove them. Any one of them moving inside the sensor cone trips the PIR.
  5. Set Movement (0–100) to choose how much the creatures move. At 0 they stand still and the PIR goes silent, even with six of them in the beam.
  6. Tune the Sensors: PIR Sensitivity (0–100%) trades detection range against false alarms, and Water Threshold (0–100%) sets how wet the floor must be to count as a leak.
  7. Read the Rover OLED. Switch between Status, Sensors, Alert and Network screens, or turn it off with Display ON.
  8. Check Sensor Telemetry: the analog Water Sensor, the Nearest and Fastest creature, PIR Output, PIR Hold Left, PIR Range, and how many Pipes are leaking.
  9. Watch Alerts Sent and Throttled climb. The station reports on its own — nobody presses anything.
  10. Press Pause to freeze everything, or Reset to start again. Change one setting at a time and compare.
  • Motion, not presence: a PIR sensor detects movement. A creature holding still is invisible to it.
  • PIR hold time: a PIR output stays HIGH for a few seconds after the movement stops.
  • Sensitivity: higher sensitivity detects from further away, but also gives more false alarms.
  • Thresholds: an analog water sensor gives a range of values, and a threshold turns it into a simple yes or no.
  • Sensors read their own spot: four leaking pipes still read dry if the rover is standing between the puddles.
  • Rate-limited alerts: IoT devices limit how often they send alerts, so they don't fire non-stop.
  • Automatic warnings: a connected device can send remote warnings with no human pressing anything.
  • Fair testing: change one setting at a time and keep the others the same, so you know what caused the difference.
