AQUANOVA ALERT STATION

PIR MOTION · WATER LEVEL · BLYNK IoT ALERTS · LOF ROBOTICS

  1. The rover patrols the habitat watching for two dangers: movement (PIR sensor) and water (water level sensor).
  2. Turn on Creature in Zone and raise Movement. Watch the PIR cone catch it — then drop Movement to 0 and see the alert die away.
  3. Raise Water Drop Level to start the pipe leaking. A puddle spreads across the floor; the rover only reads water it is standing in.
  4. Drag the rover along the floor, or leave Patrol on to let it sweep by itself.
  5. Change the Emergency Condition to see how motion only, water only, both together, and either one give completely different alarms from identical sensors.
  6. Watch the Blynk phone on the right receive alerts on its own — nobody pressed anything.
  • Why a PIR sensor detects motion, not presence — a creature holding still is invisible to it
  • Why a PIR output stays HIGH for a few seconds after the movement stops
  • How sensor sensitivity trades detection range against false alarms
  • How an analog water sensor is turned into a yes/no answer by a threshold
  • How AND / OR logic over two sensors changes what counts as an emergency
  • Why IoT alerts are rate-limited instead of firing continuously
  • How a device sends remote warnings with no human in the loop
