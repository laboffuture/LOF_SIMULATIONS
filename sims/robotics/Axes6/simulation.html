1. Pick a mode at the top of the Command Panel, then press ▶ Start on the arm view. Every mode begins with the arm in its power-on pose.
  2. Joint Test: choose one axis and move it both ways. Watch which servo channel lights up and how the gripper position changes. Tick all six axes. Try the Fault drill to see what an unplugged servo or swapped wires look like, then press Fix wiring.
  3. Command Control: each button sends one command (e.g. ELBOW_FORWARD). The joint moves by the Step size (1°, 5° or 10°).
  4. Slider Control: drag a slider to send an exact servo angle. HOME and STOP still work.
  5. Guided Pick-and-Place: follow the 12 steps on the banner under the arm. The command to use next glows blue.
  6. Precision Challenge: the object and the target outline are placed at random. Pick the object, match the outline's orientation with the wrist rotation, place it, then press HOME. No hints: just a timer.
  7. Picking: point the gripper straight down over the object, turn the wrist so the jaws close across the object's sides, open the gripper, lower onto the object, then press GRIPPER_CLOSE.
  8. Servo speed: Smooth and Normal move each servo with gentle acceleration. Fast jerks the joints: carrying an object at that speed can make it slip out.
  9. HOME sends every axis to its home angle. STOP freezes all servos where they are. ⏹ Stop ends the run and opens the result report; ↺ Reset starts the mode again.
  10. Drag the 3D view to orbit, scroll to zoom. Hover an arm part to see its servo channel.
  • Six degrees of freedom: base rotation, shoulder, elbow, wrist tilt, wrist rotation and gripper: six servos, each controlling one movement.
  • Commands to motion: a command is read by the controller, which picks the servo channel and sends it a PWM pulse. The pulse width sets the servo angle: 500 µs is 0°, 2500 µs is 180°.
  • MG996R vs MG90S: the big MG996R servos (about 10 kg·cm of torque) carry the load at the base, shoulder and elbow. The small MG90S servos (about 2 kg·cm) are light enough to sit at the end of the arm for the wrist, wrist rotation and gripper.
  • Coordinated movement: reaching needs base + shoulder + elbow together; a gentle pick needs wrist + gripper; matching a slot needs wrist rotation.
  • Home position, smooth motion and angle limits keep the arm safe: a known start, no sudden jerks that shake the object loose, and no driving a servo past its range.
