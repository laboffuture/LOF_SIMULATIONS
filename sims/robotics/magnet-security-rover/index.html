Magnetic Heading Patrol

  1. The rover starts patrolling Zone 1 on its own. Press Pause to stop it and Reset to put it back at the start of the zone.
  2. Choose Zone 1 · Habitat Corridor or Zone 2 · Research Lab. The rover loads that zone's target heading (90° east or 0° north) and drives straight along it.
  3. Move the Target Heading slider. The rover sees a heading error and turns until it faces the new direction.
  4. Watch the Magnetometer panel: the compass shows the target (green) and the current heading (orange), the X, Y and Z field values, the heading error, the rover's decision and the left and right motor speeds.
  5. Press Bump! or Rotate 30° to knock the rover off course. Watch the motors speed up on one side and the cyan trail curve back onto the heading.
  6. Raise Vibration to shake the rover as it drives. Small wobbles inside the green heading threshold band are ignored; bigger ones are corrected.
  7. Change the Heading Threshold and Correction Sensitivity, or turn off Auto-correction to see the rover drift away from its path.
  8. Click on the map to place the alien intruder (drag it to move it), or press Place Intruder Ahead.
  9. Watch the AMG8833 8×8 thermal grid warm up as the rover gets close. The status changes from Safe to Monitoring to Intruder Detected.
  10. Change the Intruder Temperature and the Temperature Threshold. The light and buzzer turn on only when the hottest pixel crosses the threshold, and stay on for the Alert Duration after the heat drops. Then the rover continues its patrol.
  • Magnetometer: a triple-axis magnetometer measures the magnetic field along its X, Y and Z axes.
  • Calculating heading: the X and Y values give the direction the rover faces: heading = atan2(Y, X).
  • Heading comparison: the controller subtracts the current heading from the target heading to get the heading error.
  • Motor correction: to turn right the left motor speeds up and the right motor slows down, and the other way round to turn left.
  • Threshold and sensitivity: errors smaller than the heading threshold are ignored; the sensitivity sets how hard the rover steers back.
  • Disturbances: bumps, vibration and uneven motors push the rover off course, so it must keep checking and correcting.
  • Thermal imaging: the AMG8833 sees heat, not a picture: 64 infrared pixels each measure a temperature.
  • Temperature threshold: a warm intruder is only reported when its heat crosses the set limit, which avoids false alarms.
  • Automatic alerts: the controller switches on the light and buzzer by itself, keeps them on for a few seconds, then lets the rover carry on.
