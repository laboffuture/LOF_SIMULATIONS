Stability Scout Rover — Terrain Balance Lab

  1. Press ▶ Start. The Stability Scout Rover appears on smooth terrain and drives by itself.
  2. Watch the QMC Heading Panel and the Stability Meter: the terrain surface type, the heading disturbance, the safe and unsafe threshold lines, the rover speed and the warning indicator.
  3. Select a terrain: Smooth, Rocky, Slope, Crack Zone or Unsafe. The rover changes its movement based on stability, not randomly.
  4. Study the stability zones: low disturbance means move fast, medium means slow down, high means stop or heavy slow, and a sudden disturbance shows a warning.
  5. Adjust QMC Sensitivity, the Safe Threshold and the Unsafe Threshold to see how much earlier or later the rover reacts.
  6. Press Place Obstacle. The IR sensors detect it and the rover stops or turns to avoid it. Press Clear Obstacle to remove it.
  7. Combine conditions, for example rocky terrain with an obstacle, and see that the rover checks both stability and obstacles before deciding how to move.
  8. Choose Full Rescue Route to run the whole mission to the rescue point before the mission timer runs out.
  9. Use Fault Injection to see the error and warning messages.
  10. Use Reset Rover, Reset Terrain, Reset Sensors or Reset Mission to repeat the experiment.
  • How QMC heading disturbance can indicate unstable movement
  • How terrain type affects rover stability
  • How safe and unsafe thresholds help classify terrain
  • Why the rover should move fast only on stable terrain
  • Why the rover should slow down on uneven terrain
  • Why unsafe terrain should trigger warning or stop behaviour
  • How IR sensors detect obstacles in the rover path
  • How obstacle avoidance supports safe navigation
  • How speed control helps prevent tipping or crashing
  • How multiple sensors can work together in a rover safety system
