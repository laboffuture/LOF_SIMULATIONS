Dragonfly Rotorcraft

Field lab · vertical flight
  1. Pick a world and a craft. Worlds: Earth, the Moon, Mars, Titan. Crafts: three test drones (Quad, Hexa, Octo) and two real NASA presets, Ingenuity (Mars) and Dragonfly (Titan).
  2. Throttle is your only control. This lab is vertical flight only, with no steering. Drag the rail on the right, or tap ▲ / ▼ (each tap is 5% of the hover RPM; hold to repeat). Keys: ↑ / ↓, and Shift for 1% steps.
  3. Hover when thrust equals weight (T = mg). The blue T arrow turns green when it matches the red W arrow, and the T/W gauge needle sits in its green zone at 1.0.
  4. Read the live equation at the bottom. Each term lights up in the world colour when it changes.
  5. Drag the bottom panel up or down. Its tabs hold Telemetry, Motor bay, Power and Airframe (mass and rotor-radius sliders).
  6. Motor bay: click a pod (or press 1–8) to fail a motor, then cut its balancing partner and see whether the craft can recover.
  7. Experiments row: work through all nine. Each one ticks off when you've done it. Tap any ? or the world label to collect field notes.

By the end you should be able to:

  • State the hover condition: thrust equals weight, T = mg.
  • Predict whether the hover RPM goes up or down when gravity (g) or air density (ρ) changes, then check it in the sim.
  • Explain why rotor radius matters so much (R⁴): doubling R cuts the hover RPM to a quarter.
  • Explain why Mars needs huge rotors, and why blade tips approaching the speed of sound set the limit.
  • Explain why flying on Titan takes about 40× less power than flying the same craft on Earth.
  • Justify NASA's choice of 8 rotors for Dragonfly using the motor-failure test.
  • Explain why no rotorcraft can fly on the Moon: with no air (ρ = 0) the rotors have nothing to push down, however low the gravity.
