Compressed-Air Rocket Dynamics & Stability

Model rockets fly on impulse boost and aerodynamic coasting. Stability is governed by the relative positions of the Center of Gravity ($X_{cg}$) and Center of Pressure ($X_{cp}$).

  1. Boost Phase: Launch pressure creates a short impulse $J = k \cdot P \cdot V$, imparting burnout velocity $v_0 = J/m$.
  2. Coast Phase: Atmospheric drag $F_d = \frac{1}{2}\rho C_d A v^2$ decelerates the rocket until peak apogee.
  3. Stability Margin ($SM$): $SM = (X_{cp} - X_{cg})/d$. If $SM < 1$, fins cannot stabilize the rocket, causing it to tumble and lose altitude!
