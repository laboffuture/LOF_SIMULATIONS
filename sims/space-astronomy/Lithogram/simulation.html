Lithogram: Belt Survey

  1. Plan the ship: pick Chemical or Ion propulsion and a propellant load. The curve shows how much propellant your delta-v budget needs.
  2. Fly the run: steer with A / D, the arrow keys, the on-screen Thrust buttons, or by holding the left or right half of the view. Every sideways move burns a little propellant.
  3. Rocks ringed in cyan are tagged. As one approaches, its free scan (brightness, colour, spectrum squiggle and probability bars) appears bottom-right. Steer into it to open the Survey Lab, or let it pass if your contract does not need that class.
  4. In the Survey Lab, fire extra instruments (Thermal IR, Spectrometer, Density probe, Radar) from your survey budget. The probability bars on the right update after each one.
  5. Commit a class (C, S or M) and a confidence, then extract: choose a tool, the strike energy and whether to anchor. Watch which fragments escape.
  6. Read the debrief and return to the run. Each sector's gate opens when its contract quota (top-right) is met. After Sector 5 you get the Field Report.
  7. Turn on any Show Equation toggle to see the model behind a readout. Open the Lab Bench to move every parameter by hand, or the Sandbox to build your own asteroid and survey it.
  • Brightness is not size: a small shiny rock and a large dark one can look equally bright. D = 1329 / √pV × 10−H/5.
  • Spectra are fingerprints: the dip near 1 micron comes from iron-bearing silicates, so it separates S-types from C-types. M-types are nearly featureless.
  • Density from an orbit: a probe skimming the surface has a period that depends only on density, ρ = 3π / (G T²). Low density means a porous rubble pile.
  • Classifying is inference: you combine readings with the base rate of the sector (Bayes' rule), and say how confident you are.
  • The belt has a gradient: S-types dominate the warm inner belt, C-types the cold outer belt. Jupiter's resonances carve gaps such as the Kirkwood gap at 2.5 AU.
  • Tiny gravity: on a 1 km asteroid the escape speed is about half a metre per second, so a hard strike throws ore away for good.
  • Fuel is exponential: the rocket equation Δv = ve ln(m0/mf) decides whether a mining plan is affordable.
