The math behind it (for the curious):
• Speed anywhere: v = √(μ(2/r − 1/a))
• Circle speed: v = √(μ/r)
• Lap time: T = 2π√(a³/μ)
What the letters mean: v = speed, r = current distance from Earth's centre, a = orbit size, μ ("mu") = Earth's gravity strength, T = lap time.

Welcome to Orbital Spin

Watch a satellite loop around Earth — and change its path yourself. An orbit is just the curved road a satellite follows as it falls around the planet without ever hitting it.

Try this:

  1. Tap a real mission (like the Space Station) to load its orbit.
  2. Drag the low point and high point sliders to squash or stretch the orbit.
  3. Press Speed up or Slow down to fire the engine and watch the shape change.

You're starting in Simple view — just height, speed, and the orbit's shape in plain words. Switch to Full anytime (top of the panel) for every readout and control. Stuck on a word? Open the Glossary.

Glossary

Orbit
The looping path a satellite follows as it falls around Earth without hitting it.
Altitude (height)
How high the satellite is above Earth's surface.
Low point periapsis
The point of the orbit closest to Earth.
High point apoapsis
The point of the orbit farthest from Earth.
Orbit shape eccentricity, e
How stretched the loop is. A value of 0 is a perfect circle; closer to 1 is a long, stretched oval.
Orbit size a
The overall size of the orbit, measured from Earth's centre.
Lap time period, T
How long one full trip around Earth takes.
Speed up prograde
Firing the engine forwards, in the direction you're moving, to go faster.
Slow down retrograde
Firing the engine backwards to go slower.
Δv delta-v
"Delta-vee" — a change in speed from firing the engine. Rockets carry a limited budget of it.
Tilt inclination, i
How tilted the orbit is compared with Earth's equator. 0° hugs the equator, 90° goes over the poles.
Escape speed
Go this fast and you break free of Earth's gravity for good.
Orbit energy
The orbit's total energy per kilogram. More negative means more tightly bound to Earth. (High-school / college physics.)
Angular momentum spin
A measure of the orbit's "spin" that stays constant while the satellite coasts. (High-school / college physics.)
Earth-bulge drift J2
Earth bulges at the equator, and that bulge slowly twists an orbit around over days. At a tilt of 63.4° the twist stops. (Advanced.)
Hohmann transfer
The fuel-cheapest way to move between two round orbits: one burn to leave, a long coast, then one burn to arrive.
