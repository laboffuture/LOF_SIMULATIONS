Mirror Maze Spectrum

  1. Light leaves the source on the left, passes through up to two elements and lands on the detector screen (bottom view).
  2. For a quick start, press a Quick Scenario: Rainbow Prism, Laser Beam, Diffraction or Bent Path.
  3. Under Light Source, set the Brightness and pick White Light (all colours) or Monochromatic (one Wavelength, 400–700 nm).
  4. Choose Slot 1 or Slot 2, then click an element in the tray (Mirror, Prism, Grating, Filter, Blocker, Diffuser or Lens) or drag it onto a slot. Remove clears a slot.
  5. Change each element's settings in its slot: the Tilt Angle for a mirror, prism, grating or lens, the Lines per mm (100, 300 or 600) for a grating, the Passband Color for a filter, and Converging/Diverging and the Focal Length for a lens. A prism also shows its Snell's law working.
  6. Auto-aim keeps the screen on the beam. Turn it off to move the screen with the Screen Angle slider.
  7. Read the line under the detector view: it names the pattern you made, such as a rainbow, a focused spot or a dark screen.
  8. Change one thing at a time and compare the results.
  • White light: sunlight-like white light is a mix of all the colours, from violet (about 400 nm) to red (about 700 nm).
  • Reflection: a mirror bounces light so that it leaves at the same angle it arrived; tilting the mirror steers the beam.
  • Refraction: light bends when it passes into glass and out again, following Snell's law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.
  • Dispersion: glass bends violet light more than red, so a prism spreads white light into a rainbow. A single laser colour does not spread.
  • Diffraction: a grating's fine lines split light into several beams called orders; more lines per mm spread them wider, and red bends the most.
  • Filters: a colour filter lets only one band of colours through and absorbs the rest.
  • Lenses: a converging lens brings light to a focus and a diverging lens spreads it out; the focal length sets how strongly.
  • Scattering and blocking: a diffuser scatters light in all directions, while a blocker stops it completely.
  • Fair testing: change one element or setting at a time to see what each one does to the light.

Mirror Maze Spectrum Guide

Interactive Optics Laboratory Workbench

Light originates from the calibrated source module on the left, travels through up to two interactive optical elements, and strikes the orbital screen detector array.

  1. Prism Dispersion: Place a Prism in Slot 1 with White Light to observe Cauchy dispersion fan out the visible spectrum.
  2. Laser Coherence: Switch to Monochromatic mode; single wavelengths do not disperse further.
  3. Ray Bending: Place a Mirror or Diffraction Grating to manipulate direction and harmonic spectral orders.
Wavelength (λ)
Measured in nanometers (nm). Human visible range spans ~380nm (violet) to ~700nm (red).
Snell's Law of Refraction
Governs light bending across medium boundaries: n1 sin(θ1) = n2 sin(θ2).
Diffraction Grating
Diffracts light into distinct harmonic spectral orders where longer red wavelengths bend furthest.
