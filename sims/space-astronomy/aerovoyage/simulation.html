AeroVoyage — Rotor Lift Lab

One drone. Four worlds. Real physics. Nothing to win — just explore.

The same drone travels to Earth, Mars, Titan, and the Moon. Build it with the controls on the right, then push the throttle at the bottom to spin the rotors and lift off.

  1. Push the throttle on Earth and watch the seesaw tip — more push than weight means it climbs.
  2. Tap Mars. The air is 60× thinner — the same drone can barely lift. Make the rotors bigger and try again.
  3. Tap Titan: thick air, weak gravity — flying is easy. That's why NASA is sending a rotorcraft there.
  4. Tap the Moon. No air at all. No matter how hard you push, it will not lift. Rotors can't push against nothing.
  5. Flip Show the math to reveal the one equation behind every seesaw, bar, and RPM number.

Real missions

Ingenuity (Mars): NASA's little helicopter had to solve exactly this thin-air problem — it spun its blades about 2,500 RPM, roughly 5× faster than a drone on Earth, just to get off the ground.

Dragonfly (Titan): NASA's upcoming rotorcraft. Titan's air is over 4× thicker than Earth's and gravity is weak, so big rotors lift easily — perfect for flying.

The Moon: No rotor drone has ever flown there, and none ever will — there's no air for the blades to push. Landers and hoppers use rockets instead.
