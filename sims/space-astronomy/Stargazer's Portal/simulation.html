Stargazer's Portal

  1. Look at the target shape card at the top left to see the constellation outline you are hunting for.
  2. Pan the sky by dragging in any direction across the main view to locate the matching group of real stars.
  3. Zoom in or out with the mouse scroll wheel (or pinch on a touch screen), or use the + / − buttons in the bottom corner. Zoom in to separate stars that sit close together.
  4. Tap the stars that form the pattern in any reasonable order. Correct taps light up and connect automatically with lines following the real constellation's line data.
  5. A wrong tap just doesn't connect — there is no timer and no penalty for wrong taps, keeping the experience low-pressure for pattern-recognition and collection.
  6. Tap the HINT button in the top corner to toggle an optional faint guide outline over the correct stars in the current view.
  7. Once all the right stars are connected, its name appears, then the constellation lifts out of the sky and twirls while a short one-line fact appears beside it.
  8. Completed shapes are collected in your star-map scrapbook at the bottom. Tap any collected constellation in the scrapbook to pop up a small card with its shape, name, and fact, and to highlight it again in the sky.

Learning Objective: The learner should come away able to recognize that constellations are patterns made from real, specific stars in the actual sky, and be able to pick out a familiar shape (like the Big Dipper or Orion) from a busy field of stars on sight.

  • Real Star Data: The star field is a simplified but real rendering using genuine star positions:
    • Right Ascension (RA): The star's position measured like longitude on the sky (0–360°).
    • Declination (Dec): The star's position measured like latitude on the sky (−90° to +90°).
    • Apparent Magnitude: How bright the star looks on a reversed, logarithmic scale (lower numbers are brighter; e.g. Sirius is about −1.46, naked-eye faint stars are around +5).
  • Flat, Pannable Sky Projection: Treating the entire sky as one large, fixed, pannable flat map using equirectangular projection where RA wraps around seamlessly at the edges (panning past 360° loops back to 0°).
  • Real Brightness Rendering: Display sizes follow the formula displaySize = baseSize × 10^(-0.2 × magnitude), making bright stars noticeably larger and faint stars subtle.
  • Difficulty Progression: As rounds advance, magnitude cutoffs progressively lower (allowing fainter stars up to magnitude 4–5 into the sky), making the correct pattern genuinely harder to pick out from background clutter.
