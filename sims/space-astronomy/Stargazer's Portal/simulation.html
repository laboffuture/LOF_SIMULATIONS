Stargazer's Portal

  1. Look at the Target Shape card at the top left. It shows the outline of the constellation you are hunting for. Its name stays hidden until you connect it.
  2. Drag the sky in any direction (or use the arrow keys) to look around and find the group of stars that matches.
  3. The readout at the bottom shows the RA and Dec under the small cross in the middle of the sky. Tap it (or press U) to switch RA between hours and degrees.
  4. Zoom with the mouse wheel, by pinching on a touch screen, or with the + and − buttons, to separate stars that sit close together.
  5. Tap the stars that make the shape, in any order. Correct stars light up, join with lines and flash their name and brightness. A wrong tap shows a quick red ring and nothing else. There is no timer.
  6. Hover over any star (or press and hold it on a touch screen) to see its name, RA, Dec and brightness. The blue pointer on the brightness scale at the bottom slides to that star.
  7. Stuck? Press Hint to show a faint outline over the right stars. Press Grid (or G) to draw the RA and Dec lines of the sky.
  8. From Round 5 the sky no longer moves to the target for you. Use the clue on the card, such as Look near RA 7h 00m, Dec +25°, with the readout and the grid. Take me there is there if you get stuck.
  9. When the shape is complete, its name appears and the constellation twirls. A card follows with a sky fact and a table of its stars; tap a row to ring that star in gold. Later rounds add one quick question. Press Next Target to hunt the next one.
  10. Each find is saved in the Star-Map Scrapbook at the bottom (18 to collect) and is kept on this device. Tap one, or step through them with the ‹ › buttons, to see its card. Tap a star's row and the sky turns to that star.
  11. On a scrapbook card, press Highlight in Sky to light that constellation up, and press it again to put it out. Each one can be lit or unlit on its own.
  12. Each find also shows its shape: the figure it is named after, drawn over its stars as in Stellarium. Use the Show shape switch on a scrapbook card to turn one on or off, or All shapes in the scrapbook bar for every find at once.
  13. Later rounds show fainter stars, so the shapes get harder to spot.
  • Constellations are real: each one is a pattern made from real, specific stars in the night sky. The background stars come from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue.
  • Spotting shapes: you learn to pick out familiar patterns, like the Big Dipper or Orion, from a busy field of stars.
  • Right Ascension (RA): a star's position measured like longitude on the sky, from 0h to 24h (or 0° to 360°). See it: the readout at the bottom of the sky.
  • Declination (Dec): a star's position measured like latitude on the sky, from −90° to +90°. See it: the readout at the bottom of the sky, and the Grid button.
  • Apparent magnitude: how bright a star looks. Lower numbers are brighter: Sirius is about −1.5, while the faintest stars you can see by eye are about +6 under a dark sky. See it: the brightness scale at the bottom right of the sky. Tap or hover over any star and the blue pointer slides to that star's brightness.
  • Brightness and size: bright stars are drawn bigger and faint stars smaller, just as they look in the real sky.
  • A sky you can turn: the sky is drawn the way you see it from Earth, so constellations keep their true shapes everywhere, even next to the pole. East is to the left, so RA increases to the left.
  • Faint stars make it harder: the limit rises from magnitude 3.4 to 4.3 to 5.0 as you go. As more faint stars appear, the shape hides among them, just like under a dark sky.
