Measuring the Cosmos

  1. Pick a scale: My map (your own guess), True scale, Textbook or Log.
  2. Press and drag on the scene: drag right for more power, left for less, and up or down to aim. The number shows the jump in AU. Your last power is kept, so retries are quick.
  3. Let go to jump. Land on the next planet. Arc over or under hazards.
  4. Each planet is a level with three stars: land, hit the bullseye, and grab the probe. Retry as often as you like.

Keyboard: hold Space to charge (like the original), ← → adjust the power, ↑ ↓ aim, let go of Space to jump.

After landing, press Next ▶ to fly on. ◀ Previous goes back a planet, ⓘ Info reopens a planet's facts, and you can click any planet you've reached on the Whole trip strip to revisit it.

Class 6–8 · CBSE/NCERT Science · The Solar System

By the end you should be able to:

  1. Order the eight planets by distance from the Sun.
  2. Describe the pattern: the four inner planets sit inside about AU, and the outer gaps keep getting much bigger.
  3. Use the AU (the Sun–Earth distance, about ) to compare distances. Neptune is about times farther from the Sun than Earth.
  4. Explain why a true-distance model doesn't fit on a screen or in a classroom.
  5. Explain what a log scale keeps (the order, and that outer planets are farther) and what it hides (the real size of each gap).
  6. Spot the “evenly spaced planets” mistake in a textbook diagram.

Teacher note. Each level zooms so the gap fills the screen, and the zoom badge says by how much. The jump reading is the real distance in AU, so the same pull reads very differently from level to level on the Textbook and Log scales. Hazards sit where the real things are (asteroid belt , Kuiper belt beyond Neptune). Sizes are not to scale, and the planets are lined up only for the game.
