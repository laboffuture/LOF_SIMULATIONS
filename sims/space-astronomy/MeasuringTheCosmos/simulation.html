Measuring the Cosmos

  1. Choose a scale from the three cards: Equal Spacing, Real Distance or Log Scale. The scale decides how far apart the planet platforms are.
  2. You begin on the Sun. Your first target is Mercury, and the goal is to reach Neptune.
  3. Charge the jump: hold the spacebar, the mouse button, or your finger on the screen. The longer you hold, the more the Jump Power bar fills and the farther you jump.
  4. Let go to jump towards the next planet. The dotted arc shows most of your path.
  5. Adjust your aim slightly with the left / right arrow keys, or drag up and down while you hold. The arrow shows the direction.
  6. Land on the next planet to carry on. Planets must be visited in order, so flying past one counts as a miss.
  7. If you fall short or overshoot, press Retry (or the spacebar) to try again from the last planet you reached.
  8. In Real Distance, some gaps are too wide for any jump. Land on the glowing booster pad and it carries you the rest of the way.
  9. The mini-map at the bottom shows the Sun and all eight planets in the scale you chose. The white marker is you.
  10. The top panel shows your scale, the planet you are on, your next target, your score and your retries. Landing near the centre and landing on the first try score more.
  11. Press Pause (or P) to restart the journey or switch scale.
  12. Reach Neptune to see the same solar system drawn in all three scales, side by side. Try every scale to earn all three badges.

You should come away able to understand:

  • that planets are not equally spaced from the Sun,
  • that the inner planets are much closer together than the outer planets,
  • and that real solar system distances are difficult to show on a normal screen or classroom model.
  • You should also understand that logarithmic scale is useful because it compresses very large distances while still preserving the idea that outer planets are much farther away than inner planets.
