1. Pick Outdoor or Indoor. The rover always turns on its own to face whatever light source is strongest — you never aim it by hand.
  2. Outdoor: press Move Sun to step the sun across the sky. Watch the rover swing around to keep facing it — for a moment right after it moves, the rover is still turning and generation dips.
  3. Indoor: drag the rover around the room. Near the window it catches daylight leaking in; turn on the Lamp and the rover will turn toward that instead if it's the stronger source.
  4. Choose the Device Load the station is powering.
  5. Press Start. Nothing else to set — the battery charges automatically whenever there's usable light, and the device runs automatically off the battery as long as it has charge.
  6. Watch the readings and energy flow.
  7. Try this: charge outdoors at Noon until the battery is full, then switch to Indoor with the lamp off and the rover away from the window — the device keeps running on what you stored, with zero light anywhere nearby, until the battery runs out.
  8. Press Reset to clear the battery and start over.
  • Solar power is only generated when a light source is actually available
  • A rover with a light sensor can automatically turn to track the strongest light
  • While it's still turning toward a new light direction, less power is captured
  • Indoors, natural light comes through windows and falls off with distance
  • A device can keep running on stored battery energy even with no light nearby
  • A heavier device load drains a battery faster
  • Charging and using power can happen at the same time — the battery is just the balance of the two
  • How energy changes form: light → electrical energy → stored energy → device power
