1. Set the Flight Conditions: pick a Preset (ready-made situations that use every part of the simulation), or build your own from the Route, Time, Ground Ice and Weather.
  2. Fly between London, Berlin, Moscow, Tokyo and Delhi. Each city has its own airport, skyline, landmarks and departures board.
  3. Only realistic conditions can be chosen: each city only offers the weather it really gets (Delhi never freezes, Tokyo has no freezing rain), and ice can only be on the aircraft when it is below freezing.
  4. The weather changes the flight: how low the icing cloud starts, how fast ice builds in it, how rough the air is, and how slippery the runway is for the take-off and the landing.
  5. Press Inspect Aircraft. The Inspection list shows the ice on the aircraft, the outside air and the moisture.
  6. Choose on the Ice Protection panel: De-Ice (the ground crew removes ice that has already formed) or No Action. Anti-Ice is built into the aircraft's wings — it cannot be used on the ground and only works in flight.
  7. Watch the take-off and flight on the Flight Instruments. Ice only forms higher up, in the icing cloud. When it starts forming, a warning and a timer appear — act before the timer runs out, or the crew makes an emergency landing at the nearest airport and the flight is not completed.
  8. Read the result and the explanation. If a decision was wrong, press Try Again; if it was right, go on to the Next Preset. Fast Forward speeds up the calm parts of the flight.
  • Instead of directly telling you which system to use, the simulation gives you a flight situation. You inspect the condition, think, and act.
  • Flow: Flight Conditions → Inspect Aircraft/Weather → Choose an Action → See System Action → Take-off & Flight → Safe Flight or Failure → Explanation
  • Core learning — discover through the presets:
    Ice already exists on the groundDE-ICE before take-off
    Clean aircraft on the groundno ground treatment needed
    Ice forming in flight, in the icing cloudact fast — the protection is built into the aircraft
