1. Watch the mission begin: a powerful storm hits the island, lightning strikes and the mobile network fails. The survivor's phone shows No Service.
  2. Turn the EMS ON by clicking the POWER rocker switch on the EMS Device. The OLED shows EMS READY and the battery level.
  3. Press one of the 3 keys on the device: 1 SOS, 2 Medical Help or 3 Food & Water (or press 1–3 on the keyboard). The OLED shows the message you picked.
  4. Choose the Battery Level (25%, 50% or 100%), the Signal Strength (Low, Medium or High) and the Weather Condition (Clear, Light Rain or Heavy Storm).
  5. Press Send Message and watch the radio signal travel from the survivor to the rescue station's antenna. Every message uses some battery, and High power uses the most.
  6. Read the result on the Rescue Station View in the right panel: Delivered, Weak Signal (delayed) or Failed.
  7. When the message gets through, the rescue team answers it: SOS — a rescuer rappels down from the helicopter, ties the survivor to their harness, both are winched up and flown back inside the station. Medical Help — the helicopter lands and two paramedics carry the survivor on a stretcher to the helicopter and into the station. Food & Water — a crate of food and water is dropped by parachute.
  8. When a rescue is complete, the scene resets automatically for the next mission. Your settings and the Observation Table are kept.
  9. Every message is saved in the Observation Table. Send messages in different weather, power and battery settings and compare the results.
  10. Press Reset to replay the mission from the storm (your observations are kept), or Reset Observation Table to clear the table.
  • Students should learn how an Emergency Messenger System can send distress messages when mobile networks fail during disasters.
  • Core idea: Storm damages towers → Mobile network fails → Survivor uses EMS → Radio signal reaches rescue team.
  • Signal logic:
    ConditionResult
    EMS OFFMessage cannot be sent
    Mobile network failedEMS radio signal must be used
    Strong signal + enough batteryMessage delivered
    Weak signal + stormMessage delayed or failed
    High signal strengthBetter delivery, but more battery use
    Low batteryMessage may fail
    Heavy stormSignal becomes weaker
    Clear weatherSignal travels better
  • Battery logic:
    Battery LevelBehaviour
    100%All messages can be sent reliably
    50%Message can be sent, but high power drains faster
    25%OLED shows LOW BATTERY warning
    0%EMS turns OFF, message cannot be sent
