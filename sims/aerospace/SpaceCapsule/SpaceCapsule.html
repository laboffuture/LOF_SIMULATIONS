What you are testing

A crew capsule is returning from orbit. You choose its size, how much it carries, its parachute, the height the parachute opens, and the wind. Then you fly the return and see whether it survives the water landing.

How it works

  • The capsule hits the air at 7.8 km/s heat-shield-down. The heat shield bleeds off almost all of that speed.
  • The parachute opens and the capsule settles almost at once to its terminal speed, set by total mass ÷ parachute area:
    speed = √( 2 · mass · g / (ρ · area · Cd) )
  • Deployment height and wind do not change that speed — they set the fall time (height ÷ speed) and the drift (wind × fall time).

Reading the result

  • Safe ≤ 5 m/s  ·  Hard 5–10 m/s  ·  Failed > 10 m/s
  • Mission Success needs a Safe splashdown inside the target zone.

Try this

  • Only the Large chute on a Light capsule lands Safe (~4 m/s) — try every payload.
  • Fly the Heavy capsule on the Small chute (~29 m/s, Failed), then swap to Large (~10 m/s).
  • Change deploy height — the splashdown speed stays the same, only Fall Time moves.
  • Put the Light + Large combo in Strong wind and watch the long, slow fall drift off target.
