Five ways to fly

  • Learn: the tutorial. Eight short lessons teach every control in detail, from moving the sticks with the plane held still to landing. A lesson card shows what to do, the key to press glows, and each step ticks when you do it.
  • Free: free practice with the practice checklist on the right.
  • Course: fly through glowing floating rings in order against the clock. The amber ring is next; an arrow on screen and a dot on the map show where it is. A missed ring adds 5 s. Three courses: Easy, Medium and Hard.
  • Tricks: aerobatics. Pick a trick to see how to fly it; the steps on the right tick off as you go and every trick is scored. Choosing Tricks sets up the Aerobat plane, Manual mode, High rates and smoke (V).
  • Aero Ace: the flight-academy game. Nine missions, from Runway Burst to the Final Aero Trial. Each mission card tells you the task; the game checks your speed, height, direction and stability, then scores Control Smoothness, Altitude Accuracy, Gate Accuracy, Wind Correction, Stall Recovery and Landing Precision. Pass a level to unlock the next one and raise your rank to Aero Ace Pilot.

Helpers

  • Watch demo (in each mode and on the start card): the autopilot flies the course, mission, trick or a full circuit while the sticks on the transmitter move, so you can see the inputs. Press any flight key, or grab a stick, to take control.
  • Path P: a thin light-yellow guide line with small diamond markers shows the route to fly (also on the map). Each marker turns green once you fly through it.
  • Assist: small automatic corrections: it keeps the wings level and holds the height when you let go of the stick, coordinates the rudder in turns, keeps you on the runway centre line, pushes the nose down before a stall and softens the touchdown. The line under the transmitter says what Assist is doing.
  • Throttle: a tap of W / S moves it 5%; holding it moves it slowly. 1 2 3 jump to Low, Medium and High. Like a real transmitter, the motor only arms when the throttle is at Low.

Before you fly

  1. Pick the Plane: the Trainer (high wing, very stable) or the Aerobat (low wing, more power, fast roll: built for tricks).
  2. Pick a Flight mode. Trainer works like a gyro-stabilised receiver on a real trainer plane: the sticks set the bank and pitch angle, and when you let go the plane levels itself. Gyro works like a stabilising receiver in rate mode: the sticks set how fast the plane rolls and pitches, and when you let go it holds whatever bank it has, with gusts damped out, but it does not level itself. Manual is a plain RC plane: the control surfaces move only while you hold the stick, and nothing levels the plane or fights the wind for you.
  3. Pick the Rates switch, like the dual-rate switch on a transmitter. Low (30% of full surface movement) is gentle and best for learning, Mid (50%) for everyday flying, High (80%) for sharp manoeuvres and tricks. Expo softens the sticks around the centre: at 40% a half stick gives about a third of the movement, while full stick still gives the full rate. Turn it up if small corrections feel twitchy.
  4. Pick the Wind, the Camera and the Time of day. Chase follows behind the plane. Pilot shows what you see standing at the flight line with the transmitter, the way real RC pilots fly; it zooms in as the plane flies away so you can still see it. FPV (first-person view) is the picture from a small camera on top of the plane, like FPV goggles. With Drag view on, drag the 3D view with the mouse to look from any angle; double-click it to go back. Morning and Sunset put the sun low, so it can dazzle you when you fly towards it.
  5. Press Start Enter.

The transmitter (Mode 2)

  • Left stick up / down = Throttle WS. It stays where you leave it, like a real throttle stick.
  • Left stick left / right = Rudder AD. Yaws the nose left or right. On the ground it also steers the nose wheel.
  • Right stick back / forward = Elevator ↓↑. Pull back (↓) = nose up. Push forward (↑) = nose down. This is how every real transmitter works.
  • Right stick left / right = Ailerons ←→. Rolls the plane to bank into a turn.
  • Rudder, elevator and ailerons spring back to centre when you let go. Holding a key moves the stick further; a short tap moves it a little.
  • Elevator trim TG sets a small, permanent amount of elevator so the plane holds its height with the right stick centred.
  • Extra keys: 123 set the throttle to Low / Medium / High, X cuts the motor, hold Shift for half-stick fine control, IJKL also work as the right stick, M mutes and −= change the volume. The Keyboard box on the left lights up each key you press.
  • You can also drag the sticks with the mouse or a finger, or plug in a USB RC transmitter or gamepad.

Take off

  1. Push the throttle to High. Keep the plane on the centre line with small touches of rudder (in crosswind the plane turns into the wind).
  2. At about 11 m/s pull the right stick back gently. The plane lifts off. Climb to about 20 m, then centre the elevator and set the throttle to Medium.

Fly

  • Turn by banking with the ailerons, then add a little up elevator to keep the nose from dropping in the turn. Level the wings to stop turning.
  • Keep the speed above the red band on the speed gauge. Too much elevator with too little speed causes a stall: release the elevator, push the nose down a little and add throttle.
  • Stay within 300 m of yourself (the amber ring on the map). At 400 m the radio link is lost and the receiver's failsafe cuts the motor.

Land

  1. Line up with the runway from far out. Throttle Low, nose slightly down, and let the plane sink gently.
  2. Just above the runway, pull back a little (the flare) so the plane touches down at less than 1.6 m/s sink. Cut the throttle and roll to a stop. The flight report opens.

Reading the screen

  • Gauges along the bottom of the flight view: speed (red band = stall speed and too fast), roll and pitch, altitude (red mark = 120 m limit), heading, and climb rate.
  • Map (top right, north up): runway, you, the 300 m and 400 m range rings, the white flight path line, the plane, and the wind arrow.
  • The coloured parts of the plane match the channel colours in the transmitter: amber propeller = throttle, teal = elevator, violet = rudder, pink = ailerons. Watch them move as you move the sticks.
  • The white line behind the plane is its flight path. The white streaks show the wind; the windsock and the club flag next to you stream the way the wind blows, and the trees and grass sway more in stronger wind.
  • The flat flying field is surrounded by crop fields, a lake, a road and hills. Trees, the lake and the hills are hazards: hitting them ends the flight.
  • Sound: the slider at the top left sets the volume of the motor, the airflow, the stall horn and the effects. The speaker button mutes.
  • An amber flashing border is a warning; a red one is a stall, lost signal or crash.
  • The Practice checklist on the right ticks itself as you show each skill. The highlighted item is your next one.

The four controls

ControlWhat it movesWhat the plane does
ThrottleMotor speed / powerFaster or slower; low power can mean losing lift
ElevatorTail surface (horizontal)Nose up / down, climb or descend
RudderTail surface (vertical)Yaws the nose left / right
AileronsWing surfacesRoll left / right to bank into a turn

Key ideas

  • Lift = ½ ρ V² S CL. Lift grows with the square of speed. Double the speed gives four times the lift. The plane flies level when lift equals weight.
  • The elevator changes the wing's angle of attack, which changes CL. Past about 14° the airflow breaks away from the wing and lift collapses: a stall. Too much elevator at low speed causes it; lowering the nose and adding power cures it.
  • Throttle mostly controls speed (thrust against drag). The elevator then turns that speed into climbing or descending.
  • A plane turns by banking: the ailerons tilt the lift sideways and that sideways part pulls the plane round. In a bank, less lift points up, so you need a little up elevator to hold height. Too much bank, or a sharp roll, means losing control.
  • The rudder yaws the nose. On a trainer with dihedral (wings angled up in a slight V) yaw also makes the plane roll, so the rudder alone can turn it, but less neatly than the ailerons.
  • The plane flies in the moving air, so wind pushes it sideways over the ground. In a crosswind you point the nose slightly into the wind (crab) with rudder and ailerons to stay on the runway line.
  • Real RC flying has rules and limits: stay under 120 m and within sight. If the radio link is lost, the receiver's failsafe cuts the motor.
  • Smooth, small, coordinated inputs on all four controls are what make a flight stable and a landing safe.
